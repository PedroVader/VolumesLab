# Resting Energy Expenditure: What It Is and How It Is Measured

Resting energy expenditure, or REE, is the energy the body uses while at rest. It supports ongoing functions such as breathing, circulation, temperature regulation, and cellular activity. REE can be estimated with an equation or measured under controlled conditions using indirect calorimetry.

A measured REE value is not the same as total daily energy expenditure. Daily expenditure also includes physical activity and the energy associated with processing food. The resting measurement describes one component of the total.

## What resting energy expenditure includes

The body continues to use energy when a person is physically inactive. Organs, tissues, and regulatory systems remain active, and REE represents the combined energy cost of that resting activity over time.

In many people, resting expenditure is a large component of total daily expenditure, but the exact proportion varies. The measurement does not record the full energy cost of a normal day unless the additional components are assessed separately.

## REE, RMR, and BMR

Resting energy expenditure and resting metabolic rate, or RMR, are closely related terms. They are often used for the same practical concept, although one can be expressed as energy over a period and the other as a rate.

Basal metabolic rate, or BMR, is defined under more restrictive standard conditions. In everyday articles and calculators, BMR and RMR are often used interchangeably, but research protocols may distinguish them according to fasting, prior activity, environmental conditions, and the period of rest before measurement.

The report should therefore state the term and protocol used instead of assuming that every resting figure was obtained in the same way.

## How indirect calorimetry measures REE

Indirect calorimetry estimates energy expenditure from respiratory gas exchange. During a resting measurement, the system records oxygen consumption and carbon-dioxide production while the person breathes through a canopy, hood, mask, or mouthpiece, depending on the equipment.

The word “indirect” refers to the fact that the system does not measure heat production directly. It uses the measured gas exchange to calculate energy expenditure through established equations.

A typical workflow has three parts:

1. The equipment is prepared and calibrated according to its operating protocol.
2. Respiratory gas data are collected while the person remains at rest under defined conditions.
3. A stable measurement period is selected and converted into an estimated resting energy expenditure, usually reported over 24 hours.

The exact interface, resting period, stability criteria, and calculation method depend on the equipment and protocol. These details should be documented on the service page or report.

## Measured REE versus a predictive equation

A predictive equation calculates a resting value from personal variables. Depending on the equation, these may include age, sex, height, weight, or estimated fat-free mass.

An equation can be useful when gas-exchange measurement is not available, but it remains a population-derived estimate. Its average performance in a group does not guarantee a close estimate for every person.

Systematic reviews have found that equation performance varies between populations and that individual error can remain even when average bias appears small. This is why a calculator result and an indirect-calorimetry result should be labelled as different methods.

| Question | Predictive equation | Indirect calorimetry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input | Personal variables and a mathematical model | Measured oxygen consumption and carbon-dioxide production |
| Setting | Calculator, app, or report | Controlled resting measurement |
| Output | Predicted resting expenditure | Resting expenditure calculated from measured gas exchange |
| Main limitation | Population model may not fit an individual closely | Result depends on equipment, protocol, resting conditions, and data stability |

## What affects measurement quality

Indirect calorimetry requires a defined protocol because recent food intake, physical activity, stimulants, rest, environmental conditions, movement, and measurement duration can influence resting gas exchange. Equipment calibration and the selection of a stable data period also matter.

This article does not set a preparation protocol. The provider's documented instructions and the exact equipment procedure should define the conditions for a specific appointment.

## What the result does and does not represent

The result represents estimated energy expenditure at rest under the conditions of the test. It does not, by itself, measure the energy used during an entire day. It also does not prescribe an intake, determine a diet, or diagnose a metabolic condition.

If a report uses the result in a broader calculation, the additional assumptions should be shown separately. This keeps the measured resting component distinct from estimates for activity and other parts of daily expenditure.
