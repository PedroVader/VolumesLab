## Reader and outcome

- Audience: adults and athletes who already have an estimate from a wearable, fitness app, or field test

- Outcome: the reader can identify the inputs, setting, output, and main limitations of each method
- Not for: diagnosis, medical clearance, training prescription, or interpretation of an individual's result

## Editorial strategy

- Direct answer: an estimate uses sensor data, performance data, and an algorithm; a laboratory CPET records respiratory gas exchange during controlled exercise. The numbers can be related but are not directly interchangeable.
- Unique angle: explain the method before discussing accuracy, and avoid describing either method as infallible.
- Critical gaps: direct definition, comparison table, reasons for differences, and a clear boundary around what the result does not show.
- Do not imitate: sensational wearable-versus-lab stories, universal accuracy percentages, or claims that one number reveals overall health.
- Structure:
  1. Direct answer
  2. What an estimated result is based on
  3. What a laboratory test records
  4. Side-by-side comparison
  5. Why the two numbers can differ
  6. How to compare repeat results without giving training advice
  7. What is confirmed about the Volumes Lab test
  8. Sources and review note
- Length: 750 to 950 words, enough to answer the comparison without becoming an exercise guide
