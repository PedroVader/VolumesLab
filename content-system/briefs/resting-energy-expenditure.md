# Article brief: Resting Energy Expenditure

## Identity

- Working title: Resting Energy Expenditure: What It Is and How It Is Measured
- Proposed URL: `/blog/resting-energy-expenditure`
- Article type: FAQ-style product explainer
- Language and market: British English, Madrid, Spain
- Primary query: resting energy expenditure
- Supporting questions: how REE is measured, indirect calorimetry, REE vs BMR, REE vs total daily energy expenditure
- Primary intent: understand the quantity and the test before considering a metabolic assessment

## Reader and outcome

- Audience: adults and athletes who have seen a predicted calorie figure or are considering an indirect-calorimetry test
- Decision: understand what a measured resting value represents and what it does not represent
- Outcome: the reader can distinguish REE, total daily energy expenditure, indirect calorimetry, and predictive equations
- Not for: calorie prescriptions, weight-loss guidance, diagnosis, or individual-result interpretation

## Editorial strategy

- Direct answer: REE is the energy used while the body is at rest. Indirect calorimetry estimates it from oxygen consumption and carbon-dioxide production under controlled resting conditions.
- Unique angle: keep the article centred on measurement and terminology, not what someone should eat.
- Critical gaps: REE versus total expenditure, gas-exchange method, measured versus predicted value, and protocol sensitivity.
- Do not imitate: calculators, universal calorie targets, diet advice, or claims that one test determines an ideal intake.
- Structure:
  1. Direct answer
  2. What REE includes
  3. REE, RMR, and BMR terminology
  4. How indirect calorimetry works
  5. Measured versus predicted values
  6. What changes the quality of a measurement
  7. What Volumes Lab currently confirms
  8. Sources and review note
- Length: 750 to 950 words
